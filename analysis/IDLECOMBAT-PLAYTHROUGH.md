# IdleCombat playthrough and fix backlog

Played the public [IdleCombat build](https://dusd24.github.io/IdleCombat/), displayed in-game as **Breach Squad**, in Chrome across November 7–8, 2026 CT. The run started with the tutorial, $0, four Riflemen slots and a 40-troop cap. I used the game’s own speed control, mostly at 3×, and sometimes used Send Now. I used a 1468 × 836 desktop viewport and a 390 × 844 responsive check in Chrome. This is a playtest and prioritization report.

**Result.** Cleared all **20 authored sections** across five bases and reached **Level 6, Operation Viper**, with Wave 122 forming, 12 slots open, and all 11 squad types unlocked. The game showed the usual “Base captured!” banner and a $2,750 reward for Iron Citadel, then automatically started the first endless line. I used that transition as the browser run’s finish line. [Operation Viper](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/36-operation-viper.jpg)

**Status labels.** Reproduced means observed in Chrome. Source-confirmed means the behavior also has a matching explanation in the local checkout. Balance evidence includes the browser run and, where labeled, three seeds of the existing campaign simulator. Ideas are proposals for a later build.

Keep those choices visible when simplifying the interface.

## Fix first

| ID | Priority | Finding and reproduction | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| BUG-01 | P1 | **Army Limit purchase is broken.** At Bunker Row, open Upgrades with over $300 and click **+10 Troops**. The balance is not charged and the cap remains 40. The mouse and keyboard both reproduce it. Chrome logs `TypeError: Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'tier')`. The local sheet assigns the generic squad upgrade handler to the Army Limit button too. [Screen 10](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/10-stale-upgrade-cash.jpg) · [Chrome error](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/chrome-army-upgrade-errors.txt) | The first purchase spends $300, immediately changes the cap to 50, updates the next price, and survives reload. |
| BUG-10 | P1 | **A save during a breach restores the defeated gate.** On Iron Citadel’s Kill Zone, I paused immediately when the banner appeared and HP reached 0, used the speed button to trigger a save, then reloaded. It stayed on Kill Zone and restored the gate to **15,759 HP**. Cash stayed **$9,271**, including the **$750** breach reward, and barracks stayed destroyed. The line had to be breached again to continue. [At 0 HP](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/29-breach-save-before.jpg) · [After reload](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/30-breach-save-after.jpg) | Commit the advance and reward together. Reload at any point in the celebration without reviving the gate, losing the advance, or paying the bonus twice. |
| BUG-02 | P1 | **The displayed army limit can be exceeded.** At 40 cap, send a new wave while survivors are still fighting. Observed 41/40 in Sandbag Line and 46/40 at Command Tent. Screen 08 shows 45/40. The simulation pauses new training starts at the limit, but a launch can still add the whole group. [Screen 08](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/08-over-army-limit.jpg) | Define whether 40 means a hard limit or a training threshold, then enforce or label it consistently. | BUG-03 | P1 | **Cash and availability freeze in the upgrade sheet while combat keeps running.** At Bunker Row the battlefield HUD read $1,056 while the open sheet still showed $920. [Screen 10](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/10-stale-upgrade-cash.jpg) | Cash, enabled actions and newly unlocked units update without reopening the sheet or resetting focus or scroll. |
| BUG-04 | P2 | **The breach recap credits waves that never hit a building.** Rusher Gate logged “Wave 10 · 0 building damage · broke through the gate.” Heavy Gate repeated it for Wave 23 after Wave 22 recorded 7,064. The local code marks every still-unreported wave as captured when changing lines. | BUG-05 | P2 | **The wave estimate divides gate HP by damage to all buildings.** It estimated 51 more waves at Heavy Gate after the first 125 building damage, then 132 more waves at Armor Gate with all three barracks still alive. Both gates were still full. The chart also estimated one more wave after Tank Yard reached 0 HP. | BUG-06 | P2 | **Reload resets the tactical state and unpauses.** At Switchback, a paused reload preserved $1,568, 8,514 gate HP, lineup, squad levels, current section and Wave 35. ## Balance backlog

| ID | Priority | Observation | Change to evaluate |
|---|---|---|---|
| BAL-01 | P1 | **The first gate teaches grinding before it teaches counterplay.** Sandbag Line starts with a 4,600-HP gate, two barracks and covered gunmen. The wall-gun tip recommends grenades and mortars. Grenadiers unlock at Level 2 and Mortars at Level 4. A shield is available from the start, but the actionable opening strategy was to upgrade Riflemen and survive until the barracks fell. | Give the first section one threat and a counter the player can actually deploy. Make the first useful spend or barracks knockout happen earlier. Teach another threat on the next line. |
| BAL-02 | P1 | **Tanks and vehicles cause the largest spikes.** The same simulator strategy took 3.7–9.1 simulated minutes on Tank Yard, and Motor Pool ranged from 3.0 to 6.0 minutes. Citadel HQ took 8.8–12.9 minutes. Averages hide the runs where several waves barely touch the gate. | Tune the vehicle depot wave, infantry escort, and arrival spacing together. Track median and slow-run time to first meaningful gate damage as well as clear time. |
| BAL-03 | P1 | **Power feedback is hard to read when reinforcements adapt to the player.** In the local rules, part of enemy reinforcement HP and damage scales with deployed lineup strength. The intel chip shows the same enemy counts after a player upgrade. This can make a paid upgrade look ineffective even if it helped. | Establish predictable baseline strength for the teaching campaign. Test clearer reinforcement tiers or reserve stronger adaptation for optional operations. |
| BAL-04 | P2 | **Send Now changes the economy of a fight.** “Ready · holding” can last while survivors chip buildings, even when there is room under the cap. Manually sending lets the player overlap those groups and speed through. Sending too early can also dispatch units that have not reached their squares. | Treat it as a deliberate reinforcement decision. Show who is being sent, who is staying, and why the automatic wave is waiting. Measure clear times with auto-only versus occasional sends. |
| BAL-05 | P2 | **Adding another copy can slow the whole lineup.** At the starter stats, Riflemen list an 8.4-second training time, but four copies share two simultaneous training jobs and still have to walk into place. Similar bottlenecks recur when doubling up on RPGs. “Trains in” is per squad, while the decision the player needs is full-wave ETA. | Surface two training lanes per squad type and an estimated next send. Compare a third copy of a specialist with a mixed squad using both time and output. |
| BAL-06 | P2 | **Late unlocks have to compete with investments made much earlier.** Slot and upgrade costs rise steadily, while every new squad starts at Lv 1. This favors upgrading a familiar core and makes a situational swap harder to judge without spending first. | Try a one-wave loaner, an onboarding upgrade grant, or a practice range for new squads. Measure whether Mortar, Breacher, Medic and Humvee each earn a repeatable job in the campaign. |
| BAL-07 | P3 | **Most of a line’s remaining HP can become a cleanup timer.** After the last barracks falls, reinforcements stop and current survivors grind down the gate. That payoff is satisfying once, but it repeats after the outcome is clear. [Screen 05](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/05-first-breach-in-progress.jpg) | Trial a short siege payoff after the final defender falls, or give the player a small post-barracks decision such as accelerating the breach versus recovering troops. |

## UI and accessibility backlog

| ID | Priority | Observation | Change to evaluate |
|---|---|---|---|
| UX-01 | P1 | **The opening tutorial is a wall of reading before the first interaction.** It also says waves launch once every square is filled, omitting the survivor hold. Early enemies can start appearing before the player has a foothold. [Screens 01 and 02](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/01-onboarding.jpg) | Teach one action at a time on the field. Highlight one starting tile, animate a real wave, then highlight the first affordable purchase. Use short verbs, icons and optional read-aloud for pre-readers. |
| UX-02 | P1 | **Important enemy intel is hidden off the right edge.** Desktop and mobile use an overflow strip. At Heavy Gate the rusher chip is clipped, and at Motor Pool the vehicle chip is initially offscreen behind static defenses. There is no visible scrollbar. [Screens 11, 12 and 15](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/12-motor-pool-transition.jpg) | Surface the threat driving the fight first. Wrap a short enemy list or add a conspicuous “more enemies” control. Give each surviving barracks a source marker and a labeled counter. |
| UX-03 | P1 | **The player’s next decision competes with chart, grid, feed, cash, and enemy timers.** The chart is present before there are results, its legend wraps into the plot area, and repeated lock prices take space from the current action. The most useful “why am I stalled?” information is in transient feed cards. [Screen 08](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/08-over-army-limit.jpg) | Put the objective, current blocker, and one suggested action in a persistent compact strip. Make wave analysis an optional drill-down with labels and units. |
| UX-04 | P2 | **The squad picker and shop look almost identical.** “Place” sits beside “Upgrade,” locked cards lengthen the scroll, and HP/DPS values do not say they are per troop. There is no before/after preview or count of slots affected by an upgrade. [Screens 03, 04 and 10](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/04-lineup-picker.jpg) | Give picking and spending separate emphasis. Add a squad-total view, before/after values, and “affects X assigned squads.” Put available counters first and group locks behind a single heading. |
| UX-05 | P2 | **Opening a square needs a second tap in about 2.6 seconds.** A later second click just re-armed the purchase. The shared price jumps from $120 to $220 after the first unlock, and the money is spent before a squad is selected. [Screen 05](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/05-first-breach-in-progress.jpg) | Make the pending action explicit in a purchase card. Show row, price and resulting lineup, with confirm and cancel controls that do not expire. |
| UX-06 | P2 | **Changing a tile can delay the forming wave.** Replacing an assigned squad mid-cycle reduces readiness, although the grid only says changes apply to the next squads trained. Local logic sends queued members of the replaced slot home. | State the cost before replacing a ready squad or offer “apply after current wave.” Keep live swaps and queued changes visually distinct. |
| UX-07 | P2 | **Mobile puts the battle and its decision tools on separate layers.** The mobile battlefield is readable, but the Lineup sheet obscures the frontline and combines intel, formation, and chart in one panel. The battlefield has no persistent shortcut to the priority threat. [Screens 14 and 15](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/15-mobile-lineup.jpg) | Keep a compact current-threat chip on the field. Let the player peek at the fight while making common changes, with a separate detail view for the chart. |
| UX-08 | P2 | **Several statuses rely on tiny text, color, or inaccessible canvas details.** Desktop sound and menu measured 36 × 36 CSS px. Unit colors and small health bars are hard to follow in clusters. The battlefield exposes one canvas description, and the speed control’s accessible name omits the selected multiplier. [Screens 08 and 14](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/14-mobile-battle.jpg) | Use shapes and labels for ready, walking, low HP and destroyed states. Include the selected speed in its accessible name. Provide accessible text equivalents for wave results and predictable focus for drag alternatives. |
| UX-09 | P3 | **The game name differs across surfaces.** The public URL and project are IdleCombat, while the title, tutorial and HUD say Breach Squad. [Screen 01](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/01-onboarding.jpg) | Use one public identity or explicitly present Breach Squad as a subtitle. |

## Functions and quality-of-life ideas

| ID | Priority | Idea | Player benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| IDEA-01 | P1 | **Campaign map, replay, and post-HQ checkpoint.** The browser run rolled straight into endless after the final capture. Show completed bases and campaign length. Add a durable victory summary and a choice to replay, stop, or start Operation Viper. [Screen 36](idlecombat-playthrough-evidence/36-operation-viper.jpg) | Gives a child a clear stopping point and makes favorite missions easy to revisit. |
| IDEA-02 | P1 | **Wave debrief and persistent event log.** Show squads sent/lost, gate/barracks/bunker damage, healing, and one causal tip. Include a compare-to-previous-wave option limited to the current line. | Turns a wiped wave into a decision. A player who stepped away can see what happened. |
| IDEA-03 | P1 | **One-tap beginner presets.** Offer an editable “Stop rushers,” “Break the gate,” or “Beat vehicles” setup using owned units. Preview changes and any spend. | Introduces composition without requiring a young player to parse eleven stat cards. |
| IDEA-04 | P2 | **Save slots or export/import.** Name a main save and a practice save. Make Start Over a real confirmation with a cancel action. | Lets a child and adult share a device and try different strategies safely. |
| IDEA-05 | P2 | **Bounded idle return.** If offline earnings are added, grant a capped amount of cash or training credit and report it at resume. Keep major campaign clears opt-in. | Rewards coming back without skipping the battle the player wanted to see. |
| IDEA-06 | P2 | **Hold and priority orders.** Add an optional deliberate hold, a clearly labeled reinforcement send, and one simple priority such as “focus vehicle depot.” | Gives a player control over wave timing and the most important target without turning the game into an RTS. |
| IDEA-07 | P2 | **Loadouts and assignment shortcuts.** Save two or three formations, show a row preview, add “replace matching slots” with confirmation, and support keyboard swap. | Cuts repetitive picker work as the grid reaches twelve slots. |
| IDEA-08 | P3 | **Settings for shared play.** Offer volume, mute, low-motion battle effects, high-contrast squads, and short tutorial prompts. | Makes the game usable beside an adult and easier to read on a small screen. |
| IDEA-09 | P3 | **Give some bases a distinct objective.** Try a marked depot raid, timed convoy defense, or turret sabotage as a short optional mission. Keep the main campaign’s gate rules easy to recognize. | Adds variety and gives C4, healing, and rapid-response units more distinct uses. |

## Suggested order

1. Fix Army Limit, breach saves and stale upgrade availability. Verify a purchase, a cap edge case, and reloads before, during and after a breach.
2. Put a playable counter and an affordable first decision on Sandbag Line. Make the blocker and next action visible without opening a sheet.
3. Split gate damage from other damage, then tune Tank Yard, Armor Gate and Citadel HQ using median and slow-run timings. Check both auto-only and occasional Send Now.
4. Add the campaign finish screen, replay and lineup presets. Use the debrief and persistent log to explain why formations work.

## Simulator cross-check

I ran the existing `tools/campaign.js` with seeds 1, 2 and 3. Each finished the 20 authored sections in **70.8–72.8 simulated minutes**. Seed 1 also completed Operation Viper in another 21.0 simulated minutes. The harness uses automated purchases and changes the army limit in the simulation directly, so it cannot detect BUG-01 and these times are separate from the Chrome run.

| Checkpoint | Simulated clear time across the three seeds |
|---|---:|
| Sandbag Line | 4.7–6.0 min |
| All of Dusty Outpost | 12.3–16.6 min |
| Motor Pool | 3.0–6.0 min |
| Tank Yard | 3.7–9.1 min |
| Armor Gate | 4.0–6.8 min |
| Citadel HQ | 8.8–12.9 min |

The middle of the campaign has several sub-three-minute lines. The first line and late vehicle or HQ encounters are the clearest pacing outliers for this strategy. Keep the satisfying barracks knockouts and visible gate damage when tuning them.

The Chrome run reflects one save with live changes, manual sends, and two reload tests. The mobile findings come from a Chrome viewport check. Kid-readability suggestions are design judgments from the playthrough.
